# An open letter on the Bonzo Lend recovery

**To:**
**Hedera Hashgraph, LLC**, doing business as Hedera Council — a Delaware limited liability company
**Hedera Foundation SEZC** — 190 Elgin Avenue, George Town, Grand Cayman KY1-9001, Cayman Islands

*c.c. Hashgraph Foundry Inc., Delaware*

**From:** Users of Bonzo Lend · **Date:** 21 November 2026

---

We write as users of Bonzo Lend — the protocol that, on Hedera's own case-study page, "positions itself as the liquidity layer on Hedera".[9] We do not dispute the cause of the exploit. We are writing about what followed it: a promise, approved by the parties who made it, that was not kept.

On 16 July, Bonzo published — and the main Hedera account endorsed in its own post[2] — a commitment that affected users would receive "advances covering the full value of the losses to their affected positions", "backed by a recovery facility committed by Hedera Foundation".[3]

On 12 November, 124 days later, the recovery portal opened at 81.39% aggregate, with outcomes as low as 56.1%, 658 of 2,466 accounts still short, and $1,065,255 outstanding. The Hedera Foundation's contribution was 795,431 USDC — 8.78% of the $9,061,020 taken.[1][5][12]

Bonzo has stated that the July post was "edited and approved by the Hedera Foundation and Hedera", and that "no loss calculations were provided to them (or were asked to be provided) before that post was approved and published."[4] That is an honest admission, and it is the whole problem: full recovery was promised to the market before anyone had costed it.

We are not arguing that DeFi carries no risk, or that 81.39% is nothing — most exploits return zero, and Bonzo kept the protocol paused rather than drained and published every transaction on-chain.[10] We are asking organisations that all still exist and all still benefit from this ecosystem to answer for the gap between the sentence they approved and the number they delivered.

We ask:

1. Publish the terms of the recovery facility — amount, conditions, expiry — and say who approved the July wording without a cost estimate.
2. Fund the remaining $1,065,255. It is 12.9% of the single institutional claim waived in this process, and roughly ten times the $100,000 offered for the return of the stolen funds.[7]
3. Publish the per-account claim calculations, and the terms on which the $8,261,438.83 institutional claim was waived.[5]
4. Confirm on the record who funded the 795,431 USDC. It reached the redemption contract from Circle's USDC float, so the chain proves the receipt and not the payer.[13] We accept Bonzo's attribution; one sentence naming the source would let everyone else verify what we take on trust.

Supra: the guard that would have stopped this was live on eleven other chains between 29 June and 3 July, and reached Hedera six hours after the exploit. No advisory described that rollout.[6] Explain the exclusion, and contribute. The users who were drained have received nothing from you.

Hedera's own case study is titled "Enabling decentralized credit markets on Hedera with Bonzo".[9] The people who supplied that liquidity were early to a network that tells institutions it is the trust layer of the digital economy; they produced the TVL and the network fees that made the DeFi story credible. When the oracle failed, they were left with 81.39%, a waiver to sign, and a funder already winding down.[8]

The users of Bonzo Lend are, to a large part, Hedera's DeFi layer. Treating that layer as an expense rather than as the thing the network is built on is a choice. We are asking you to make a different one.
