# An open letter on the Bonzo Lend recovery

**To:** Hedera (Hashgraph) · the Hedera Council · Hedera Foundation · Bonzo Finance Foundation · Bonzo
Finance Labs FZCO · Supra (Entropy Foundation)

**From:** Users of Bonzo Lend

**Date:** 21 November 2026

---

We are writing as users of Bonzo Lend — the lending protocol that Hedera's own case study describes as
"the liquidity layer on Hedera".

We are not writing to assign blame for the exploit. The root cause is established, acknowledged by the
vendor, and not in dispute. We are writing because of what happened afterwards: a commitment was published
in July, approved by the parties who made it, and then not met — and no one has explained the difference.

## What happened

On 11 July 2026 at 00:51:39 UTC, an attacker submitted a price update to Supra's on-chain oracle verifier.
The verifier accepted a submission carrying a zero-value BLS signature and wrote an inflated SAUCE price
to the chain. Eight seconds later, against 250 SAUCE of collateral, the attacker borrowed 6,634,528 USDC
and 34,518,389 WHBAR — $9,050,125 at the reference price of $0.06998. Roughly $5.25M was bridged to
Ethereum and laundered through Tornado Cash. Nothing was recovered from the attacker.

Supra's incident report describes this as "a cryptographic edge case". On-chain analysis published by
Protos on 15 July indicates that the same guard had been shipped to eleven other chains between 29 June and
3 July, and was applied to Hedera roughly six hours after the exploit.

## What was promised

On 16 July, Bonzo Finance published an announcement stating that affected users would receive:

> "advances covering the full value of the losses to their affected positions, calculated on the basis of
> their respective values immediately before the start of the exploit … backed by a recovery facility
> committed by Hedera Foundation."

At 15:24 UTC that day, the main Hedera account posted that announcement to its own followers with the words
"Please see an important update from @bonzo_finance". It was not a retweet. It was Hedera's own endorsement,
in Hedera's own post.

## What was delivered

On 12 November — 124 days later — the recovery portal opened with:

- 81.39% aggregate recovery against pre-exploit net entitlements
- individual outcomes between 56.1% and 100%
- 658 of 2,466 eligible accounts still short
- a remaining shortfall of **$1,065,255**

The Hedera Foundation's contribution was **795,431 USDC** — 8.79% of the $9,050,125 that was taken.

Bonzo's community lead stated on 3 November that the July announcement was "edited and approved by the
Hedera Foundation and Hedera", and that "no loss calculations were provided to them (or were asked to be
provided) before that post was approved and published."

We accept that as an honest admission. It is also the whole problem: a full-recovery facility was announced
to the market before anyone had calculated what full recovery would cost.

## What we are asking

1. **Publish the terms of the recovery facility** — the amount, the conditions, the expiry — and say who
   approved the July wording without a cost estimate.
2. **Fund the remaining $1,065,255.** It is 12.9% of the single institutional claim that was waived in this
   process, and about ten times the $100,000 offered for the return of the stolen funds.
3. **Publish the per-account claim calculations**, and the terms on which the largest institutional account
   — $8,261,438.83 at pre-exploit prices — waived its claim.
4. **Supra: explain why the Hedera deployment was excluded** from the June–July rollout, and contribute to
   the shortfall. You offered $100,000 for the return of stolen funds. The users who were drained have
   received nothing from you.
5. **Explain who inherited the Hedera Foundation's obligations.** Bonzo states that the Foundation's
   wind-down at the start of 2026 was "sudden", and that "the work, roles, responsibilities didn't cleanly
   transition over to a different org".

## Three things we are not claiming

We are not claiming users were entitled to be made whole regardless of risk. DeFi carries risk, most
exploits return nothing, and 81.39% within four months is better than the industry norm.

We are not claiming Bonzo did not try. It kept the protocol paused rather than drained, published every
sweep, conversion and sale on-chain, and returned in-kind assets with their staking value intact.

And we are not claiming the white hat was underpaid. At the snapshot he held a net position of $20,277; he
retained assets worth about $105,000. Our criticism there is narrower: the same signature that paid him also
released his claim, that was disclosed publicly only on 12 November, and the 10% he received came out of the
recovered pool — from the users — rather than from Bonzo, Supra or the Foundation, none of whom contributed
to it. A 10% bounty is fair. Paying it with other people's money, without asking them, is not.

## And one thing we are claiming

Every decision that determined how much users would receive was taken in private. The funder's commitment
was never published as a document. The distribution methodology appeared on day 111. The calculation basis
was described as "private". Third parties "require non-disclosure". The decisive $8.26M waiver was
negotiated bilaterally, on terms never disclosed.

When a user asked directly, on 30 October, who was paying for the bounty and whether losses were being
socialised across all users or only across those whose assets were rescued, the reply on 1 November
answered four numbered questions — and not that one.

Bonzo's own community lead has since described precisely this question as "a thoughtful way for the
community to use their voice to ask Hedera, Hedera Foundation, etc. why there exists a discrepancy between
what was written / approved in the July blog posting vs what was committed." We agree. That is the question
this letter puts.

A protocol that exists because of decentralisation made every material decision in this recovery the way a
bank does.

## Why this matters more than a percentage

Hedera's own case study calls Bonzo "the liquidity layer on Hedera". The people who supplied HBAR, HBARX,
USDC and SAUCE to that layer are not speculators who arrived last month. They were early to a network that
has spent years telling institutions it is the trust layer of the digital economy. They supplied the
liquidity that produced the TVL, the utilisation and the network fees that made the DeFi story credible.
When Hedera needed a flagship, Bonzo was it — and its users were introduced as the evidence.

When the oracle failed, those same users were left with 81.39%, a waiver to sign, and a funder that had
already begun to wind down.

We are not asking for sympathy. We are asking organisations that all still exist, all still publish, and all
still benefit from this ecosystem, to honour a sentence they approved in July — or to explain to the people
who provided the liquidity, in public and with numbers, why they will not.

The users of Bonzo Lend are, to a large part, Hedera's DeFi layer. Treating that layer as an expense rather
than as the thing the network is built on is a choice. We are asking you to make a different one.
